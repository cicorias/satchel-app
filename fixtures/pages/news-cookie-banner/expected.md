Overdue fines are gone for good at all eleven county branches after a trial showed more books came back, not fewer.

![Shelves of books at the Millbrook branch library](https://img.valleyherald.example/2026/10/library-stacks-800.jpg)

*The Millbrook branch saw returns rise during the pilot. Staff photo*

The county library board voted 6-1 on Thursday to make permanent a pilot program that stopped charging fines on overdue books, DVDs, and audiobooks. The change takes effect on Nov. 1, and any outstanding fines on existing accounts will be cleared the same day.

During the twelve-month trial, the share of items returned within two weeks of their due date rose from 81 percent to 88 percent, according to a report presented to the board by deputy director Anselm Kirby. The number of blocked accounts, which had kept some families from borrowing at all, fell by nearly two thirds.

"We were collecting about forty thousand dollars a year in fines and spending a fair share of that chasing them," Kirby said. "The fines were keeping the people who most need a library card away from it."

Items that are more than sixty days overdue will still be marked as lost, and borrowers will be asked to pay for a replacement. That charge is removed once the item comes back.

The lone vote against came from trustee Willa Brannigan, who said she supported the goal but worried about the budget gap. The board plans to cover it with a small increase in the county's materials allocation next year.
