The best portable power stations of 2026, tested

By Linnea Castellanos, senior reviewer, who has been testing camping and backup power gear for the site since 2019.

We test every product ourselves. When you buy through links on this page, we may earn a commission, which never affects our ratings or the order of this list.

We ran six portable power stations through the same week of tests: a fridge for twenty-four hours, a laptop and a router through a simulated outage, and a kettle to see which ones could handle a short spike. Two of them surprised us, and one that looks great on paper fell over in the cold.

All six use lithium iron phosphate cells, which last far longer than older chemistries and are safer to keep in a garage. The differences now are in inverter quality, how quickly they recharge, and how honest the capacity on the box turns out to be.

Our pick: Harbor 1000

The Harbor 1000 delivered nearly ninety percent of its rated capacity, recharged from empty in under an hour, and kept its fan quiet enough to sleep next to. It is not the cheapest per watt-hour, but it is the one we would buy for a house that loses power a few times each winter.

Safety: Never charge a power station inside a closed vehicle in summer, and keep it off carpet while it is charging.

The Pebble 300 is fine for charging phones on a weekend away but cannot run anything with a motor for long. Skip it if backup power is the main reason you are shopping.
