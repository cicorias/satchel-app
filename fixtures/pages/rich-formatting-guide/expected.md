# Winter pruning for beginners

Winter is the best time to prune most fruit trees and many deciduous shrubs. With the leaves gone you can see the shape of the plant, the sap is low, and there is not much else to do on the plot. The trick is knowing which plants to leave alone, because a few of them flower on the wood you are about to cut off.

## Before you start

You need three tools, and they need to be <mark>sharp and clean</mark>. Blunt blades crush stems, and dirty ones spread disease from one plant to the next.

- Bypass secateurs, for anything up to the thickness of a pencil
	- Not anvil secateurs, which crush
	- Clean the blades with a little surgical spirit between plants
- A pruning saw for larger branches
- Loppers, if you have an old orchard or a lot of shrubs

## The order of cuts

Whatever the plant, work through it in the same order. This is sometimes called the *three Ds* rule, followed by shaping.

1. Remove anything **dead**.
2. Remove anything **diseased**, cutting back into healthy wood.
	1. Look for cankers, which are sunken, cracked patches of bark.
	2. Burn or bin the prunings; do not compost them.
3. Remove anything **damaged** or rubbing against another branch.
4. Only then, thin and shape for an open centre.

### Where exactly to cut

Cut just above an outward-facing bud, at a slight angle that sheds rain away from it. Leave about 5 mm above the bud. On larger branches, cut just outside the collar, the slightly swollen ring where the branch meets the trunk.

> The best advice I ever got was to take out one big branch rather than fifty little ones. The tree stops sulking a lot sooner.

## What to leave alone

Plums, cherries, and other stone fruit should **not** be pruned in winter, because silver leaf disease spreads easily through cuts made in cold, wet weather. Prune them in summer instead. The same goes for spring-flowering shrubs such as forsythia: cut them right after they flower, or you lose this year's blossom.

Older guides recommended sealing large cuts with ~~wound paint~~. Current advice is to ~~paint every cut~~ leave cuts open to the air, which heal faster on their own.

It is a common myth that pruning in the 1<sup>st</sup> week of a frost harms the tree. Very hard frosts can split freshly cut wood, so wait for a mild spell above about 0 °C. Trees absorb CO<sub>2</sub> slowly in winter either way.
