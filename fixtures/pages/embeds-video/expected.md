# Hand-cut dovetails in under an hour

[YouTube video](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Xq3vB9dLm2k)

Through dovetails have a reputation for being the joint that separates beginners from everyone else. They are not that hard. What they need is a sharp saw, a sharp chisel, and a willingness to accept that your first three will have gaps you could post a letter through.

In the video I cut a single corner in pine, start to finish, with the camera on the bench so you can see exactly where the saw goes. Below are the main steps, plus a close-up clip of the part most people get wrong.

## The steps

1. Mark the baseline on both boards with a cutting gauge set to the thickness of the stock.
2. Lay out the tails with a sliding bevel at about one in seven, and saw them on the waste side of the line.
3. Chop out the waste from both faces, stopping halfway each time so the baseline stays crisp.
4. Transfer the tails to the pin board with a knife, then saw and chop the pins.

## Close-up: chopping to the line

[Video](https://www.craftcorner.example/media/dovetail-closeup.webm)

Keep the chisel vertical, and take the first cut a hair away from the gauge line. The last paring cut sits in the line itself, which gives you a clean shoulder without crushing the fibres.

If your joint is too tight to assemble, pare the pins, never the tails. The tails are your reference, and you marked the pins from them.
