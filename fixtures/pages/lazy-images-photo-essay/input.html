Autumn on the Ridgeway

Words and photographs by Bram Holloway

I had walked parts of the Ridgeway before, always in summer, always in a single afternoon. This time I wanted to do a longer stretch in autumn, when the beech woods turn and the track is quiet enough that you can go an hour without meeting anyone.

The first day was all chalk and wind. The path runs along the top of the downs, and on a clear morning you can see three counties. On this particular morning I could see about forty metres, and most of that was a sheep.

By the second afternoon the cloud lifted and the woods lit up. I stopped far too often to take pictures and arrived at the hostel after dark, which is a mistake I make on every trip and will presumably make again.

The third day was the muddiest. A week of rain had turned the lower sections into a long brown puddle, and the farm vehicles had made it worse. I walked the last six miles mostly on the grass verge, which is allowed, and mostly in silence, which was necessary.

If you go in October, take gaiters, a map that works without signal, and more food than you think you need. The pubs along the route keep shorter hours once the summer walkers have gone.
