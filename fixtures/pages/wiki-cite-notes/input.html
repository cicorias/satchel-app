The Brasswick Kettle Bridge is a cast-iron footbridge over the River Kettle in Brasswick, Northmarsh. It was opened in 1874 and is the oldest surviving iron bridge in the county.[1] It was closed to traffic in 1931 and has carried pedestrians only since then.

History

[edit]

A timber bridge stood on the site from at least the early eighteenth century. After it was carried away in the flood of 1869, the town council commissioned an iron replacement from a foundry in the neighbouring county, paid for by a toll on carts.[2] The toll was abolished in 1902.

The bridge was restored in 1998, when its deck was replaced and the original railings were recast from surviving patterns.[1]

Design

The bridge has three shallow arched spans of about fifteen metres each, resting on two stone piers. Each span is made of six cast ribs bolted together, with decorative spandrels in a pattern of overlapping rings that gave the bridge its local nickname, the Chain.[3]
