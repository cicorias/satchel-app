Fixing a 1970s desk lamp

Posted on 12 July 2026 by Teodor in repairs, with tags for lamps, soldering, and secondhand finds

Short version: it works.

I bought this lamp at a car boot sale for three pounds because the shade was a lovely shade of mustard and the seller swore it had worked last Christmas. It had not worked last Christmas, or probably for several Christmases before that.

The first job was the flex. The original cable had gone stiff and the rubber cracked when I bent it, which is a good reason not to plug anything in until it is replaced. I used a modern three-core braided flex in a similar brown and fitted a proper cord grip at the base, which the original never had.

Next was the switch in the lamp head. It was a tiny rotary switch with a spring that had rusted into a single lump. A replacement from an electronics shop was the wrong size, so I cleaned the old one with contact cleaner, made a new spring from a ballpoint pen, and reassembled it under a magnifier while holding my breath.

Finally the bulb holder. It takes a small Edison screw bulb, so I fitted a warm LED that runs cool enough not to cook the paint inside the shade. The lamp now sits on my workbench and gives exactly the right pool of yellow light for soldering in the evening.

Total cost, including the flex, the bulb, and the three pounds: just under twelve pounds. Total time: two evenings, one of which was spent looking for the spring I dropped.
