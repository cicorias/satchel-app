Why I still keep a paper notebook

Aug 3, 2026

Every January since 2020 I have tried a new note-taking app, and every February I have gone back to a cheap pocket notebook with a rubber band around it. This year I decided to stop pretending that the next app will be the one, and to write down why the notebook keeps winning.

The first reason is speed. A notebook is open before a phone has finished recognising my face, and there is no moment where I have to decide which folder, tag, or template a half-formed idea belongs in.1 I write it down, and the sorting happens later, if it happens at all.

The second reason is that paper forgets nothing and remembers nothing. It does not resurface an old note at a clever moment, but it also never syncs a blank page over a full one. I have lost exactly one notebook in six years, in a taxi, and I still think about the train timetable sketches that were in it.2

What I still do digitally

None of this means the apps lost entirely. Anything I need to search, share, or keep for more than a year gets typed up on Sunday evenings. That weekly transfer turns out to be the most useful part of the system, because it forces me to reread the week and throw away most of it.

If you want to try it, buy the cheapest notebook you can find. An expensive one makes you want to write only good ideas in it, and that defeats the purpose.

  1. I once spent ten minutes designing a tagging scheme for a note that said “buy lightbulbs”. ↩

  2. If you found a grey notebook in a taxi in the spring of 2023, the sketches were of a branch line that closed in 1962. ↩
