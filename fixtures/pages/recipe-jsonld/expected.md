# Brown Butter Oat Scones

These are the scones I make when there is half a bag of oats in the cupboard and nobody wants porridge. Browning the butter first gives them a toasty, almost caramel flavour, and the oats keep the inside tender while the edges go crisp.

The trick is to chill the browned butter until it is solid again, then rub it into the flour like you would with cold butter. If you skip the chilling you get flat, greasy scones, which taste fine but look sad.

## Tips for success

- **Toast the oats** in a dry pan until they smell nutty. It only takes four minutes.
- **Do not overmix.** Stop as soon as the dough comes together.
- **Freeze before baking** if you want to bake them straight from the freezer on a weekday morning.

I like them warm with salted butter, but the maple glaze makes them sweet enough to serve with coffee in the afternoon.

## Brown Butter Oat Scones

Nutty, crisp-edged scones with toasted oats and a maple glaze.

Prep 20 minutes Cook 18 minutes Servings 8 scones

### Ingredients

- 115 g unsalted butter
- 120 g rolled oats
- 250 g plain flour
- 50 g light brown sugar
- 1 tbsp baking powder
- ½ tsp fine salt
- 150 ml cold buttermilk
- 1 large egg

#### For the glaze

- 60 g icing sugar
- 2 tbsp maple syrup

### Instructions

1. Melt the butter in a small pan and cook until it smells nutty and the solids turn brown. Pour into a bowl and chill until firm, about 30 minutes.
2. Toast the oats in a dry frying pan over medium heat until golden. Let them cool.
3. Mix the flour, oats, sugar, baking powder, and salt. Rub in the chilled butter until the mixture looks like coarse crumbs.
4. Whisk the buttermilk and egg, then stir them in with a fork until just combined.
5. Pat into a 20 cm round, cut into eight wedges, and bake at 200°C for 16 to 18 minutes.
6. Whisk the icing sugar and maple syrup and drizzle over the cooled scones.

### Notes

Unbaked wedges keep in the freezer for a month. Add three minutes to the baking time.
