Ferry operator orders four electric vessels for island routes

By Oskar Lindqvist, North Press

This story was originally published by North Press and is republished here under a content-sharing agreement.

Oct. 20, 2026

The company that runs ferries to the outer islands has ordered four battery-electric vessels from a shipyard on the east coast, in a deal that would retire most of its diesel fleet before the end of the decade.

Each boat will carry up to sixty cars and three hundred passengers and recharge at the dock during the twelve-minute turnaround, the operator said in a statement on Monday. Charging equipment will be installed at both terminals on the busiest route first, with the remaining two routes to follow once the second pair of vessels arrives.

"Fuel is our largest single cost after wages, and it is the one we can least predict," chief executive Marguerite Osei said. "Electricity at the dock is cheaper and far more stable."

The order is expected to cost about $210 million. Roughly a third of that will come from a federal clean-transport grant awarded last spring, with the rest financed through bonds. Island residents have raised concerns about reliability in winter, when ice and high winds already cancel several sailings a month.

Osei said each vessel will keep a small diesel generator as a backup for the first five years of service.
