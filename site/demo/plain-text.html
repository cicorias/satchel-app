The Quiet Revolution of Plain Text

Why notes written in plain Markdown outlast every app they were written in.

Every few years a new notes app promises to organize your life. Every few years, its format becomes a museum piece. The notes that survive are the ones written as plain text: readable today, readable in twenty years, and readable in any editor you'll ever own.

Formats outlive apps

A Markdown file is just characters. There is nothing to export, no database to migrate, and no company that has to stay in business. If you can open a text file, you can read your notes.

The best file format is the one you never have to think about again.

Structure without ceremony

Markdown gives you just enough structure:

  • Headings to outline an idea.
  • Lists for steps and checklists.
  • Links that still work when you move the file.
  • Code blocks that keep their spacing.

Add a little YAML frontmatter at the top and every note carries its own metadata:

title: The Quiet Revolution of Plain Text
tags: [writing, markdown]
saved: 2026-10-23

A small habit

Save what you read as plain text, tag it, and keep it somewhere you control. Months later, search finds it in a second, and nothing about it depends on the app that saved it.
